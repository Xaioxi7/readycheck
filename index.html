About ReadyCheck

Please read this before your first check. You will only see it once.

What it does

A short reaction test while the camera looks at your eyelids, to help you understand how alert you are before duty.

It is not a medical device

It does not diagnose anything and does not decide whether you are fit to fly. That decision stays with you and your airline's normal process.

Camera

The camera is used only during a check. Video is analysed on this phone and is never recorded, stored or sent.

Your results

Results are saved on this phone. Nothing is shared with anyone unless you choose to share it, and you can delete everything in Settings.

How it works

1. Reaction test (primary measure)

The standard 3-minute brief Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT-B; Basner, Mollicone and Dinges, 2011), as used on tablets in daily air medical operations (Mollicone et al., 2023): a millisecond counter appears every 1 to 4 seconds (including 1 second of feedback); responses under 100 ms or with no counter showing are false starts; responses of 355 ms or more are lapses. The test always runs the full 3 minutes. Results are grouped by lapses plus false starts: 6 or fewer, 7 to 16, more than 16 (Basner et al., 2022).

Touchscreens add delay. Following NASA's touchscreen PVT validation, 77 ms (the delay measured on an iPad) is subtracted from every touch response. Real phones differ, so a production version would calibrate each device. The 3 practice tries are not scored.

2. Eyelids (supporting measure)

MediaPipe Face Landmarker tracks eyelid landmarks during the reaction test. It records PERCLOS (share of time eyes are at least 80% closed) and long closures of 500 ms or more. In a 2025 study, PERCLOS was the most informative facial feature for predicting PVT performance (Subramaniyan et al., Sleep). Eye signs can raise concern by one level but never lower it.

That study filmed at 240 frames per second. Browsers usually give 30, so short blinks and eyelid speed cannot be measured here.

3. Your own baseline

After 5 checks, your median reaction time is compared with your own history. The PVT shows no practice effect, which makes personal baselines practical. Comparing with yourself also cancels out your phone's screen delay.

4. Feeling vs measured

People under ongoing sleep loss often stop noticing how impaired they are (Van Dongen et al., 2003). When your rating and the test disagree, ReadyCheck tells you. It never tells someone who feels exhausted that they are fine.

Limitations

  • Not validated. The thresholds come from laboratory studies, mostly of total sleep deprivation.
  • Moderate fatigue, such as one poor night, is harder to detect.
  • PVT-BA was validated after the fact, not in real time.
  • The fatigue forecast is illustrative only. A real product would use a validated model such as SAFTE-FAST.
  • This is a reference for the pilot, not a fit-to-fly decision.
